# Theory audit for the meeting report

Audit scope: `multi_target_scaling_latex/body.tex`, `utility/res_rescaled.py`, `utility/envelope.py`, and `envelope_method/signed_envelope.tex` (including its mathematical appendix). No full LWC runs or new numerical experiments were performed for this audit.

## Main conclusions

1. **The link already generalizes with small changes.** Set N=n+1, calibration mean m and population-divisor standard deviation s. The augmented sample standard deviation is sqrt(s²+(z−m)²/N), because augmented variance uses divisor n. The self-score g(e)=[n(e−m)/N]/sqrt(s²+(e−m)²/N) is strictly increasing with range (−n/sqrt(N),n/sqrt(N)). Its signed inverse is −∞ below that open range, m+Nsc/sqrt(n²−Nc²) inside, and +∞ above. Preserve the sign when solving the squared equation. Removing the original max(0,·) and changing the lower outer branch from 0 to −∞ yields an exact inverse for finite residuals. The original zero branch is a conservative convention at nonnegative zero, not an exact empty-set inverse in the presence of atoms.

2. **Original full LWC is separated, not exact coupled LWC.** The paper's full-cell transformation is max_j{t_j/r_j(h_j)−c_j}, where r is the minimum scale on the local cell and c is the minimum augmented mean/scale ratio on the whole GWC coordinate. Full LWC takes each cell quantile, intersects with that cell, unions cells, then encloses coordinatewise. The alternative max_j sup_{z in I_j} F_j(t_j,z) is sharper, but should be explicitly named an exact coupled full-cell variant. Envelope uses a coordinate slab rather than a full cell and need not coincide with that exact variant. Do not call the old row shortcut the exact full-ratio full LWC.

3. **Pointwise domination is valid for nonnegative scores.** On an active cell, F(t,z) <= t/inf_I sigma − inf_G mu/sigma when t>=0. Off-coordinate envelope suprema range over G and are bounded by t/bar_s−c, with bar_s the minimum sigma over G. If G clips the mean, bar_s is sigma(project_G m), not automatically the observed s. The mean cell, when it intersects G, contains that projection after clipping. Thus each envelope row score is no larger than the old row score. Quantile monotonicity, increasing link, matching cell clipping, and upper enclosures prove C_env subset C_old. If the old method falls back to GWC, envelope containment in GWC proves the comparison. Nonnegativity is genuinely used; applying the old scale bound unchanged to negative t reverses an inequality.

4. **Validity is through pathwise full-conformal containment.** At the true candidate, augmented location and scale are symmetric functions of all n+1 observations, so scores are exchangeable. Weak rank acceptance gives coverage at least r/(n+1). A fully accepted candidate lies in GWC. For each coordinate choose its containing cell; its surface score bounds every candidate-specific calibration score. Hence its self-score is below that cell quantile and its coordinate lies in the accepted piece. This proves C_full subset C_env subset C_GWC without assuming exchangeability of the test-input-dependent envelope calibration scores on their own. Known CQR lower domains at test x are allowed even if calibration scores at other x lie below those domains.

5. **Coordinate supremum formula checks out.** The derivative is −[s²+(t−m)(z−m)]/(N sigma(z)^3). Only t>m has an interior maximum, at z*=m−s²/(t−m), with value sqrt((t−m)²/s²+1/N). For t<m, the critical point is a minimum. Evaluate finite endpoints or limits ±1/sqrt(N) and admit the maximum only when z* lies in the interval. Do not substitute zero for a missing critical entry; all endpoint scores can be negative.

6. **Search is justified without unconditional binary-search monotonicity.** The rightmost surviving closed cell has the largest surviving upper endpoint: any earlier cell ends no farther right than that cell starts. Backward search is exact. Prefix/suffix off-coordinate maxima cost O(nd), each cell O(n) with rank selection, sorting O(dn log n), worst case O(dn²+dn log n). The optional rank localization in the existing note is valid only in its certified range, not an unconditional acceptance-prefix assertion. A short report can present backward search and omit rank acceleration.

## Strictness and equality

A fully analytic example needs no experiment. For d=1, n=3, residuals (1,2,3), alpha=1/2, the rank is 2, m=2, s²=2/3. The GWC median is sqrt(3/20), so U=2+2/sqrt(21). The last cell is [2,U]. Exact-envelope median score is 0 (median residual 2 and decreasing F(2,z) on that cell), yielding bound 2. The separated median is sqrt(6)−sqrt(27/20), whose linked endpoint exceeds U. Therefore the old bound is U and the envelope bound is 2; the ratio of absolute-residual interval lengths is 2/U≈0.8209. Closed-cell conventions retain the singleton at the median. This small sample is only an analytic illustration, not evidence for a practical average effect size. Equality occurs when r=n+1, since both return the whole domain; strict local score inequalities also need not survive quantile selection and clipping.

## Signed fixed shifts versus changing-width shifts

For a fixed training-only vector b providing a true global lower bound E>=−b, translating every score and candidate by b leaves F invariant and translates omega by b. A raw CQR domain [−h(x),∞) then maps to [b−h(x),∞) subset [0,∞). Smaller-domain GWC and surface suprema are no larger than the matching old separated suprema; the resulting cells are clipped/refined pieces of the old cells. Thus the raw signed envelope is theoretically contained in the old fixed-shifted outcome region, subject to exact matching conventions. The finite group design's b_j=max_g h_gj supplies a valid bound because those are all attainable groups.

The observation-dependent shift E(x,y)+h(x)=|y−midpoint(x)| is different. It changes calibration pairwise differences and scale estimates, so translation equivariance no longer applies. Nor does the same-score theorem apply to cap=max(E,0). No general dominance is claimed versus either of these competitors. Calibrating a shift from the minimum observed calibration score would not provide a distribution-wide residual lower bound and should not be silently treated as valid.

## Report caveats and source issues

- The old manuscript uses some strict inequalities and assumes distinct residuals; the new closed treatment includes atoms. State the comparator precisely. Existing old numerical jitter can enlarge thresholds; mathematical dominance is for the explicit exact rules, with paired implementation checks as separate evidence.
- The old source's backward-search branch calls its local transform without the stored global constant, making it at least as conservative at the score level as that same cell's intended global-constant separated score. It also uses differing strict/weak endpoint tests. Do not equate every line of the executable with the closed mathematical rule without noting numerical/branch conventions.
- The old `mean_index_solver` atom/tie fix is necessary for mean-containing rows; the report's audit history already documents this. There is no reason to repeat its full debugging history in the meeting text.
- The theorem concerns the row shortcut, not arbitrary historical code or every possible enclosing full-LWC definition. The original paper's row-reduction lemma is cited/reviewed as such; the present dominance proof works directly with the explicit row scores and does not require an independent reproof of that lemma.
- Positive observed scales suffice for all formulas; population moment assumptions are unnecessary for the conditional finite-sample rank proof. For zero scales, a whole-domain fallback contains a symmetric augmented-score reference; apply the same fallback to both implementations when interpreting empirical comparisons.
- Uniform improvement means weakly smaller valid sets. A nested smaller set cannot have larger paired coverage than its superset. Neither universal strict reduction nor runtime dominance follows.

The companion `tex/theory.tex` is a self-contained four-section report body with the above definitions, construction, coverage proof, uniform containment proof, and analytic strictness example. It requires standard `amsmath`, `amssymb`, and `amsthm` packages and defines uniquely named theorem environments.
